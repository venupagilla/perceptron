1. The Golden Triangle △

These three formulas are the FOUNDATION of this entire chapter. Master these first!

Distance = Speed × Time

Speed = Distance ÷ Time  |  Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Think of D = S × T like a triangle: Cover the letter you want to find — the other two show the operation. D on top, S × T below. Memorise this triangle!

2. Units & Their Symbols

NEVER mix units! Always check before solving.

Speed:

  • km/hr — kilometres per hour
  • m/s — metres per second
  • mph — miles per hour

Distance:

  • km, m, cm, miles, feet

Time: h, min, s

If speed is in km/hr and distance in metres — convert first! Nothing will work otherwise.

3. Unit Conversions ⭐⭐⭐

Most tested concept in exams! Memorise these cold:

km/hr → m/s : × 5/18

m/s → km/hr : × 18/5

"5/18 to go DOWN, 18/5 to go UP"
km/hr (bigger) → m/s (smaller) = multiply by smaller fraction 5/18
m/s (smaller) → km/hr (bigger) = multiply by larger fraction 18/5

4. Average Speed ⭐⭐

Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time

Case 1 — Equal DISTANCE, Different Speeds

If you travel same distance at speeds S₁ and S₂ (e.g., go & return):

Avg Speed = 2 × S₁ × S₂ ÷ (S₁ + S₂)

This is the Harmonic Mean — NOT arithmetic mean!

Classic trap! Avg speed ≠ (S₁ + S₂)/2 when distances are equal.
✓ Use 2S₁S₂/(S₁+S₂)
✗ (S₁+S₂)/2 is WRONG here
A man goes to his office at 30 km/hr and returns at 20 km/hr. Find avg speed.
Step 1: Avg = (2 × 30 × 20) / (30 + 20) = 1200/50
= 24 km/hr ✓
Note: (30+20)/2 = 25 is WRONG!
⚖️ Harmonic Mean Visual

Case 2 — Equal TIME, Different Speeds

If you travel for same time at speeds S₁ and S₂:

Avg Speed = (S₁ + S₂) / 2

Arithmetic Mean — only when TIME is equal!

Exam Tip: Read the question carefully — "half the distance" vs "half the time" give different formulas! Don't mix them up!

5. Relative Speed

Relative speed = speed of one object as seen from the other object.

Same Direction

|S₁ − S₂|

Subtract the speeds

Opposite Direction

S₁ + S₂

Add the speeds

Think of it like this:
• Same direction → they're chasing each other → speed difference matters → SUBTRACT
• Opposite direction → they're coming towards each other → both contribute → ADD
🔄 Relative Speed Visual
Two trains 100m and 150m long run at 45 km/hr and 36 km/hr in opposite directions. Time to cross?
Step 1: Rel Speed = 45 + 36 = 81 km/hr
Step 2: Convert = 81 × 5/18 = 22.5 m/s
Step 3: Total length = 100 + 150 = 250m
Step 4: Time = 250 ÷ 22.5
≈ 11.11 sec ✓
For train problems: ALWAYS convert speed to m/s since lengths are in metres!
Forgetting to add BOTH train lengths when they cross each other — always use (L₁ + L₂)!

6. Train Problems 🚂

Very common in exams. Key idea: when a train crosses something, it must cover its own length + the object's length!

Case A — Train crosses a Pole / Person / Tree

Time = Length of Train ÷ Speed

Only train's length matters — pole has negligible width

A 100m train crosses a pole in 10 sec. Find speed in km/hr.
Step 1: Speed = 100/10 = 10 m/s
Step 2: Convert: 10 × 18/5 = 36 km/hr
Speed = 36 km/hr ✓

Case B — Train crosses a Platform / Bridge / Tunnel

Time = (Train Length + Platform Length) ÷ Speed

🌉 Train Crossing Platform

Case C — Two Trains crossing each other

Opposite Direction

T = (L₁+L₂)/(S₁+S₂)

Same Direction

T = (L₁+L₂)/(S₁−S₂)

140m train at 54 km/hr overtakes a 160m train at 36 km/hr. Time to overtake?
Step 1: Relative speed = 54 − 36 = 18 km/hr = 5 m/s
Step 2: Total length = 140 + 160 = 300m
Step 3: Time = 300/5
= 60 sec ✓
In overtaking problems, same direction means SUBTRACT speeds. In crossing problems, opposite direction means ADD speeds. Don't mix them!

7. Boats & Streams 🚤

When a boat moves in a river, the stream (current) either helps or opposes it.

Key Terms

  • Downstream = Boat moves with the current → Faster!
  • Upstream = Boat moves against the current → Slower!

Downstream Speed = Boat Speed + Stream Speed

Upstream Speed = Boat Speed − Stream Speed

Finding Individual Speeds

If you know Downstream (D) and Upstream (U):

Boat Speed = (D + U) / 2

Stream Speed = (D − U) / 2

🚤 Boat & Stream
A boat goes 36 km downstream in 3 hrs and 36 km upstream in 6 hrs. Find boat & stream speeds.
Step 1: D = 36/3 = 12 km/hr  |  U = 36/6 = 6 km/hr
Step 2: Boat = (12+6)/2 = 9 km/hr
Step 3: Stream = (12−6)/2 = 3 km/hr
Boat = 9 km/hr, Stream = 3 km/hr ✓
Downstream is ALWAYS faster than upstream. If your answer shows otherwise, you've made a mistake!
Mixing up formulas! Remember:
✓ Downstream = Boat + Stream (with current = faster)
✗ Downstream = Boat − Stream ← WRONG!

8. Races 🏃

Distance-based Race

If A beats B by 'x' metres in a race of 'D' metres — when A finishes D, B covers only (D − x).

Speed Ratio A : B = D : (D − x)

Time-based Race

If A beats B by 't' seconds — when A finishes, B takes t more seconds.

Speed Ratio A : B = (T + t) : T

T = time taken by A

In a 100m race, A beats B by 10m and B beats C by 20m. By how much does A beat C?
Step 1: A:B = 100:90 = 10:9
Step 2: B:C = 100:80 = 5:4
Step 3: A:C = (10×5):(9×4) = 50:36 = 25:18
Step 4: When A runs 100m, C runs (18/25)×100 = 72m
A beats C by 28m ✓

9. Circular Track Problems 🔄

Opposite Direction

Meet = C/(S₁+S₂)

Same Direction

Meet = C/|S₁−S₂|

🔄 Circular Track (400m)
First meeting time = Circumference ÷ Relative Speed
Second meeting = 2 × Circumference ÷ Relative Speed

10. Escalator / Moving Walkway

When a person walks on a moving escalator, effective speed changes!

Going UP: Effective = Person + Escalator

Going DOWN: Effective = Person − Escalator

Man walks up a moving escalator in 20 sec. Coming down takes 40 sec. His speed = 3 m/s. Find escalator speed.
Let escalator speed = e, length = L
Up: L = (3+e)×20  |  Down: L = (3−e)×40
(3+e)×20 = (3−e)×40 → 60+20e = 120−40e → 60e = 60
e = 1 m/s ✓

11. Meeting Point Problems

Two people start from opposite ends and walk towards each other.

Time to meet = Distance / (S₁ + S₂)

Meeting point from A = S₁ × Time

📍 Meeting Point Visual
Distance ratio = Speed ratio!
When time is same, distance covered is proportional to speed. So A's distance : B's distance = S₁ : S₂.

12. Speed Changed by Percentage

Speed ↑ by x%:

New Time = Original × 100/(100+x)

Time saved = Original × x/(100+x)

Speed ↓ by x%:

New Time = Original × 100/(100−x)

Extra time = Original × x/(100−x)

A man increases speed by 25% and reaches 30 min early. Find usual time.
Method: Usual time × 25/(100+25) = 30
Usual time × 25/125 = 30 → Usual time = 30 × 5
= 150 min = 2.5 hrs ✓
When speed increases, time decreases (and vice versa) — they are inversely proportional when distance is constant!

14. Golden Rules 🏆

  • Rule 1: Always convert to same unit system first
  • Rule 2: For avg speed, use Harmonic Mean for equal distances
  • Rule 3: Trains: Add lengths when crossing each other
  • Rule 4: Boats: Downstream is ALWAYS faster than upstream
  • Rule 5: Races: Speed ratio = Inverse of time ratio
  • Rule 6: Draw a diagram for complex problems

15. Common Mistakes to Avoid ❌

1. Using (S₁+S₂)/2 instead of 2S₁S₂/(S₁+S₂) for avg speed with equal distances
2. Forgetting to add train lengths when crossing
3. Not converting km/hr → m/s for train problems
4. Adding speeds in same direction (should subtract!)
5. Mixing up Downstream/Upstream formulas
6. Using wrong units (km/hr with metres)

Navneet Sir's Special Insights ⚡

Speed = Efficiency 🔗

Core Insight: Speed is the same concept as Efficiency in Time & Work.

Efficiency = Work done per unit time = Speed

  • In Time & Work: Efficiency is measured in work/day
  • In Speed: Efficiency is measured in distance/hour
  • Both mean: output per unit time
If you understand Efficiency, you already understand Speed! A person who is "twice as efficient" walks at twice the speed. This insight helps solve many problems intuitively.

Why D = S × T? (Unit Cancellation)

Speed = Distance / Time. So Distance = Speed × Time.

Unit Cancellation Trick:
When you multiply km/hr × hr, the "hr" cancels out:
(km/hr) × hr = km ✓
This is why multiplication works — units must cancel!
If speed is in m/s and time in hours, you CANNOT multiply directly! The units don't cancel. Always convert first!

Unit Conversion "Lift" Method 🛗

Think of a lift moving between floors:

Kilo → Hecto → Deca → Meter → Deci → Centi → Milli

Going DOWN (to smaller) → Multiply by 10 each step
Going UP (to larger) → Divide by 10 each step

Memory Trick — "King Henry Died By Drinking Chocolate Milk":
Kilo → Hecto → Deca → Base → Deci → Centi → Milli
The Base is meters, litres, or grams.
ConversionStepsFactor
km → mDown 3 steps× 1000
m → cmDown 2 steps× 100
cm → mmDown 1 step× 10
m → kmUp 3 steps÷ 1000

Fractional Hours to Hours & Minutes ⏰

Convert 15/2 hrs to hours and minutes.
Step 1: 15 ÷ 2 = 7 remainder 1
Step 2: 7 hours
Step 3: 1/2 × 60 = 30 minutes
15/2 hrs = 7 hrs 30 min ✓
Convert 85/4 hrs to hours and minutes.
Step 1: 85 ÷ 4 = 21 remainder 1
Step 2: 21 hours
Step 3: 1/4 × 60 = 15 minutes
85/4 hrs = 21 hrs 15 min ✓

Hours = Numerator ÷ Denominator
Minutes = (Remainder ÷ Denominator) × 60

In exams, time is often given as fractions like 15/2, 85/4. Convert to hrs & min for the final answer. Never leave answers as improper fractions!

Advanced Concepts by Navneet Sir 🎯

Same Start Time Condition ⏱️

Critical Rule: The relative speed formulas only work when both persons start at the SAME TIME!

Same direction: Rel Speed = |S₁ − S₂|
Opposite direction: Rel Speed = S₁ + S₂

✓ Works ONLY when start time is same!

Classic Trap! If A starts at 9 AM and B starts at 10 AM — you CANNOT use relative speed directly!
First find how far A goes alone in 1 hour, then use relative speed for the remaining distance.
A starts at 9 AM (40 km/hr), B starts at 10 AM (60 km/hr) from same point. When does B catch A?
Step 1: By 10 AM, A covers = 40 × 1 = 40 km
Step 2: Relative speed = 60 − 40 = 20 km/hr
Step 3: Time to catch = 40/20 = 2 hrs after 10 AM
B catches A at 12:00 PM ✓

Late/Early Distance Formula 📐

When a person is late at one speed and early at another:

D = (S₁ × S₂) / (S₁ − S₂) × ΔT

ΔT = time difference (late − early) in hours

Walking at 5 km/hr (7 min late), at 6 km/hr (5 min early). Find distance.
Step 1: ΔT = 7 − (−5) = 12 min = 0.2 hr
Step 2: D = (5 × 6) / (6 − 5) × 0.2
Step 3: D = 30/1 × 0.2
D = 6 km ✓
Late is positive, Early is negative. So ΔT = late − (−early) = late + early!

Without vs With Stoppage 🚌

Stoppage time/hr = (Without − With) / Without × 60 min

Without stoppage speed = 54 km/hr, With stoppage speed = 45 km/hr. Find stoppage time per hour.
Step 1: Difference = 54 − 45 = 9 km/hr
Step 2: Time lost per hour = 9/54 × 60 = 1/6 × 60
= 10 min per hour ✓
Trick: The speed loss (difference) tells you how much distance is lost per hour. Convert that lost distance at original speed → time lost = (Difference / Without) × 60 minutes.

LCM Method for Unknown Distance 🧠

When neither speed nor distance is given, but travel times are known:

1. LCM of travel times = Total Distance
2. Distance ÷ each time = respective speeds
3. Use standard formulas

A takes 6 hrs, B takes 8 hrs to cover same distance. Find ratio of speeds and time to meet when walking towards each other.
Step 1: LCM of 6 and 8 = 24 km (assume total distance)
Step 2: Speed A = 24/6 = 4 km/hr, Speed B = 24/8 = 3 km/hr
Step 3: Ratio A:B = 4:3
Step 4: Meeting time = 24/(4+3) = 24/7
Ratio = 4:3, Meet in ≈ 3.43 hrs ✓
The LCM method is extremely powerful when absolute values aren't given. You can assume any convenient distance — LCM makes calculations clean!

17. Practice Problems 📝

Q1: A cyclist covers 1200m in 3 minutes. Can he beat someone riding at 25 km/hr?
Step 1: Speed = 1200/180 = 6.67 m/s
Step 2: Convert: 6.67 × 18/5 = 24 km/hr
No — 24 < 25 km/hr ❌
Q2: Walking at 5 km/hr, a man misses his train by 7 min. At 6 km/hr, he reaches 5 min early. Find distance.
Step 1: Time diff = 7 + 5 = 12 min = 0.2 hrs
Step 2: d/5 − d/6 = 0.2 → (6d−5d)/30 = 0.2
Step 3: d/30 = 0.2 → d = 6
Distance = 6 km ✓
Q3: Two stations 465 km apart. Train from B at 10 AM (65 km/hr), Train from A at 11 AM (35 km/hr). When do they meet?
Step 1: By 11 AM, Train B covers = 65 km
Step 2: Remaining = 465 − 65 = 400 km
Step 3: Rel speed = 65 + 35 = 100 km/hr
Step 4: Time = 400/100 = 4 hrs after 11 AM
They meet at 3:00 PM ✓
Q4: A man travels ⅓ of distance at 10 km/hr, next ⅓ at 20 km/hr, last ⅓ at 60 km/hr. Find avg speed.
Apply: For equal distances with 3 speeds: Avg = 3abc/(ab+bc+ca)
= 3×10×20×60 / (10×20 + 20×60 + 60×10)
= 36000 / (200 + 1200 + 600) = 36000/2000
= 18 km/hr ✓
Q5: A train 240m long passes a bridge 360m long in 40 sec. Find its speed in km/hr.
Total dist: 240 + 360 = 600m
Speed = 600/40 = 15 m/s = 15 × 18/5
= 54 km/hr ✓
Q6: A man can row at 8 km/hr in still water. River flows at 3 km/hr. Find time to go 30 km downstream and back.
Down: 8 + 3 = 11 km/hr → Time = 30/11 = 2.727 hrs
Up: 8 − 3 = 5 km/hr → Time = 30/5 = 6 hrs
Total: 2.727 + 6 = 8.727 hrs
≈ 8 hrs 44 min ✓
